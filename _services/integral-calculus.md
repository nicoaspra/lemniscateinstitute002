# layout: course
summary: While differential calculus focuses on questions involving instantaneous values or rates of change, others ask a different question, “How much in total?” Integral Calculus is concerned with answering this question by studying accumulation, area, and overall effect. It provides a way to determine quantities that result from continuous change. This course develops the concept of integration as the inverse of differentiation and as a method for measuring accumulated quantities. Students explore indefinite and definite integrals, basic techniques of integration, and selected applications involving areas and totals. Emphasis is placed on understanding the meaning behind integration, supported by graphical interpretation and problem solving, so that students can view integral calculus as a natural extension of earlier mathematical ideas rather than a collection of isolated techniques.
# Integral Calculus is a branch of mathematics concerned with the theory and applications of integrals and integration. Some of its applications are determining areas under a curve, volumes of solids of revolution, and lengths of curves. The integral corresponds to summing infinitesimal pieces to describe the continuous region.  In other words, integral calculus is concerned with the process or idea of breaking down into parts to compute the whole. The term part is the infinitesimal piece derived from the concept of differentials. As you may have already noticed, Integral Calculus complements the principles of Differential Calculus.
