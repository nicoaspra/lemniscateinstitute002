# Objective
This activity introduces students to the process of shaping and finishing a machine key. Using a <strong>shaper machine</strong>, students will produce a straight key from tool steel. The key will then be finished and sized using a <strong>surface grinder</strong>. Students will also learn to <strong>round the key tips</strong> through precision grinding to achieve a proper fit inside the keyway.

# Materials and Equipment
The following materials and tools are essential for successfully completing this activity. Ensure they are available, complete, and in proper working condition before starting.
- Tool Steel Blank (Key Stock)
- Shaper Machine
- Surface Grinder with Magnetic Chuck
- Vernier Caliper
- Bench Grinder or Abrasive Wheel
- Safety Goggles
- Ruler or Steel Rule
- Vise or Clamping Setup


# Procedure
## Initial Setup and Workholding
<ol>
    <li>Cut the <strong>tool steel blank</strong> slightly longer than the final key length to allow for finishing operations.</li>
    <li>Mount the blank securely in the <strong>shaper machine's vise</strong>, ensuring firm clamping.</li>
    <li>Align the blank so that one face lies flat and parallel to the ram stroke direction.</li>
</ol>

## Shaping the Key Profile
<ol start="4">
    <li>Set the shaper to an appropriate stroke length and feed rate.</li>
    <li>Begin shaping to flatten one face of the blank.</li>
    <li>Rotate the key $90\degg$ as needed and repeat the process to machine adjacent faces.</li>
    <li>Shape until the desired <strong>key thickness and width</strong> are nearly achieved.</li>
    <li>Use a <strong>vernier caliper</strong> to measure the dimensions after each pass to avoid overcutting.</li>
</ol>

## Surface Grinding for Finishing
<ol start="9">
    <li>Place the shaped key on the <strong>surface grinder's magnetic chuck</strong>.</li>
    <li>Lightly grind the faces to reach the specified final <strong>thickness and width</strong>, ensuring accuracy and squareness.</li>
    <li>Use fine passes to improve surface finish and dimensional tolerance.</li>
</ol>

## Rounding the Ends
<ol start="12">
    <li>Mark the required <strong>tip radius</strong> on both ends of the key.</li>
    <li>Carefully shape the rounded ends using a <strong>bench grinder or abrasive wheel</strong>.</li>
    <li>Ensure both ends are evenly rounded for a proper sliding fit into the keyway.</li>
</ol>

## Final Inspection
<ol start="15">
    <li>Clean the finished key and measure all critical dimensions using a <strong>vernier caliper</strong>.</li>
    <li>Test the key for fitment in both the shaft and flange keyways.</li>
    <li>Perform final deburring or adjustments if necessary.</li>
</ol>
