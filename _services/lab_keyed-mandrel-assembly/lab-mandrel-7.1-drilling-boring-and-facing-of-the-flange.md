# Objective 
This activity introduces students to the precision operations of <strong>drilling, boring, and facing</strong> on a flange. Through this task, students will learn to accurately <strong>locate the hole center</strong>, safely perform <strong>through-drilling</strong>, enlarge the hole via <strong>boring</strong>, and achieve a smooth surface finish by <strong>facing the flange face</strong>.

# Materials and Equipment
The following materials and tools are essential for successfully completing this activity. Ensure they are available, complete, and in proper working condition before starting.
- Workpiece (AISI 1020, CRS $\varnothing$50.8 mm $\times$ 30 mm)
- HSS or Carbide Facing Tool
- Center Drill
- Twist Drills (varying sizes)
- Boring Bar
- Parting Tool
- Vernier Caliper
- Lathe Machine
- Chuck Key
- Safety Goggles
- Drill Chuck
- Drill Chuck Key









# Procedure
## Locating and Drilling the Center Hole
<ol>
	<li>Rough cut the <strong>bar stock</strong> to a length equal to twice the flange thickness, plus an additional machining allowance (approximately 5--10 mm), since the workpiece will be parted into two separate flanges.</li>
	<li>Mount the <strong>workpiece</strong> securely in the chuck. Ensure it is clamped firmly to prevent slippage during the drilling operation.</li>
	<li>Use a <strong>center drill</strong> to spot the hole at the center of the flange face. This initial hole ensures accurate alignment of the twist drill.</li>
	<li>Drill a <strong>pilot hole</strong> using a small-diameter twist drill to establish the initial depth and alignment.</li>
	<li>Gradually enlarge the hole using incrementally larger twist drills until reaching the <strong>largest available twist drill</strong> that closely approaches the desired final diameter. Drill through the full length required for both flanges.</li>
	<li>Perform a <strong>test fit</strong> using the <strong>threaded section of the shaft</strong> (the first workpiece). If the hole does not yet fit the shaft, proceed to the boring step; otherwise, you may skip it.</li>
</ol>

## Boring to Final Diameter
<ol start="7">
	<li>Mount the <strong>boring bar</strong> securely on the tool post and align it with the center of the drilled hole.</li>
	<li>Gradually enlarge the hole using <strong>light boring passes</strong>.</li>
	<li>After each pass, <strong>measure the hole diameter</strong> using a vernier caliper.</li>
	<li>Stop boring once the internal diameter matches the <strong>design specification</strong>.</li>
</ol>

## Facing the First Surface
<ol start="11">
    <li>Position the <strong>facing tool</strong> square to the face of the workpiece.</li>
    <li>Set the <strong>crossfeed zero reference</strong> and feed the tool evenly across the face to produce a smooth, flat surface.</li>
    <li>Continue facing until the desired surface finish is achieved.</li>
    <li>Reposition the tool bit or switch to a grooving tool if necessary.</li>
    <li>Machine the specified <strong>face relief</strong> based on the specification, leaving a narrow <strong>outer rim</strong> as the contact surface.</li>
</ol>

## Parting the Flange
<ol start="16">
	<li>Mount the <strong>parting tool</strong> square to the workpiece and ensure it is set at the correct center height.</li>
	<li>Position the tool to match the desired flange thickness. Add a small allowance (approximately 0.5-1.0 mm) for final facing after parting.</li>
	<li>Set the spindle speed to a <strong>low RPM</strong> suitable for parting operations.</li>
	<li>Apply <strong>cutting fluid</strong> if available, and slowly feed the parting tool into the workpiece.</li>
	<li>Continue feeding until the first flange is fully parted from the bar stock.</li>
	<li>After parting, <strong>lightly face</strong> the new end of the bar to prepare for machining the second flange.</li>
</ol>

## Facing the Opposite Side
<ol start="22">
	<li>Remount the parted flange with the unfinished side facing outward.</li>
	<li>Use the twist drill (still mounted in the tailstock) as a visual guide to approximately center the flange.</li>
	<li>Face the surface evenly until the <strong>final specified thickness</strong> is achieved.</li>
	<li>Repeat the same process for the second flange.</li>
	<li><strong>Measure the final thickness</strong> and visually inspect the <strong>surface quality</strong>.</li>
</ol>

## Final Measurements
<ol start="27">
	<li>Use a <strong>vernier caliper</strong> to measure and verify the final dimensions of each machined flange.</li>
	<li>Record your measurements and <strong>compare them with the design specifications</strong> in Table 7.1 (in the Laboratory Worksheet).</li>
</ol>
