# Objective
This activity aims to develop precision turning skills using a center-to-center setup. Students will learn to align the lathe centers, properly mount the workpiece with a lathe dog, and perform controlled turning to achieve a target diameter. The focus will be on using the crossfeed dial for consistent material removal, maintaining a reference diameter, and executing a final finishing cut to meet dimensional specifications.

# Materials and Equipment
The following materials and tools are essential for successfully completing this activity. Ensure they are available, complete, and in proper working condition before starting.
- Workpiece
- HSS or Turning Tool
- Safety Goggles
- Vernier Caliper
- Lathe Machine
- Drive Plate
- Lathe Dog
- $60\degg$ Dead Center
- $60\degg$ Live Center
- Chuck Key
- Tool Post Key / Allen Wrench %(depending on the machine)


# Procedure
## Lathe Setup and Workpiece Mounting
<ol>
	<li>Attach the <strong>drive plate</strong> to the lathe spindle.</li>
	<li>Insert a <strong>$60\degg$ dead center</strong> into the drive plate and a <strong>$60\degg$ live center</strong> into the tailstock.</li>
	<li>Ensure that the <strong>dead center is aligned with the spindle center</strong>. If misaligned, lightly cut the dead center surface to maintain the <strong>$60\degg$ angle</strong>.</li>
	<li>Approximately check the <strong>alignment of the lathe centers</strong> by bringing the tailstock center close to the spindle center without a workpiece in between (see Figure 4.1a).</li>
	<li>If the centers are misaligned, use the <strong>set over screws on the tailstock</strong> to align it with the dead center (see Figure 4.1b).</li>
	<li>Secure the workpiece by attaching a <strong>lathe dog</strong>, ensuring that it fits properly onto the <strong>drive plate</strong>.</li>
</ol>

## Initial Turning to Uniformity
<ol start="7">
	<li><strong>Zero the crossfeed dial</strong>. This will serve as the reference for all depth cuts. <strong>Do not reset this reference point throughout the operation</strong>.</li>
	<li>Begin <strong>rough turning</strong> to achieve a <strong>uniform diameter</strong> along the entire length of the workpiece.</li>
	<li>Observe and <strong>record the dial readings</strong> after each cut to track material removal accurately.</li>
	<li>Using a vernier caliper, <strong>measure the workpiece diameter</strong> after rough turning. This will be your <strong>reference diameter</strong>.</li>
	<li>Note that if your centers are aligned, straight turning will produce a <strong>uniform diameter</strong> (see Figure 4.3).</li>
</ol>

## Precision Turning to Final Dimension (First Side)
<ol start="12">
	<li>Begin by machining the side that <strong>will not be threaded</strong> (the thicker part of your workpiece).</li>
	<li>Compare your <strong>measured reference diameter</strong> with the <strong>design specification</strong>.</li>
	<li>Compute the <strong>amount of material to be removed</strong> to achieve the design specification.</li>
	<li>Use the <strong>crossfeed dial</strong> to set precise depth cuts, ensuring a controlled material removal process.</li>
	<li>Leave a <strong>thin allowance for a final finish cut</strong> to improve surface quality and accuracy.</li>
	<li>Perform the <strong>final finish cut</strong>.</li>
	<li><strong>Measure and verify</strong> the diameter using a vernier caliper to ensure dimensional accuracy.</li>
</ol>

## Chamfering the Ends
<ol start="19">
	<li>Set the tool at the <strong>required angle for chamfering</strong> and ensure it is securely positioned.</li>
	<li><strong>Use the crossfeed dial</strong> to measure and control the tool movement, ensuring an <strong>even chamfer</strong> until it reaches the planned dimension.</li>
	<li><strong>Measure and verify</strong> the chamfer dimensions using a vernier caliper to ensure accuracy.</li>
</ol>

## Precision Turning to Final Dimension (Second Side)
<ol start="22">
	<li>Remove the workpiece and detach the lathe dog.</li>
	<li>Rotate the workpiece and attach the lathe dog to the opposite end. Use a <strong>soft metal shim</strong> (e.g., aluminum can strip) to prevent damage to the workpiece (see Figure 4.4).</li>
	<li>Repeat the turning process, following the specifications on your plan.</li>
	<li>Perform chamfering on this side as well.</li>
</ol>

## Final Measurements
<ol start="26">
	<li>Use a Vernier caliper to <strong>measure and verify the final dimensions</strong> of the workpiece.</li>
	<li>Record the final dimensions and <strong>compare them with the project specifications</strong> (see Table 4.2 in the Laboratory Worksheet).</li>
</ol>
